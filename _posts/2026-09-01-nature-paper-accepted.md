Our study of a two-stage randomized public-health intervention in 110 Honduran villages
asked a question that evaluation designs usually assume away: what happens to the network
itself. In low-dosage villages, treated individuals tend to sever health-advice ties with
untreated alters. In high-dosage villages, treated individuals add ties in both directions.
Friendship and financial ties move too.

The implication is a design one. If the network is the medium through which an effect
propagates, and the intervention changes that medium, then an analysis that holds the
network fixed is describing a counterfactual that did not happen.
