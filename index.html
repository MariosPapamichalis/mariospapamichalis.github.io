I am a statistician working on data about people who are connected to one another. My research develops statistical and causal models for social networks, with attention to settings where an intervention changes not only behaviour but the social structure that sustains it.

I am a Postdoctoral Associate at the Human Nature Lab and the Yale Institute for Network Science, working with Professor Nicholas A. Christakis on large-scale sociocentric network studies and randomized interventions in rural Honduras. I hold an MPhil and PhD in Statistics from University College London, and I previously I collaborated with Professor Edoardo M. Airoldi and held a postdoctoral position at Purdue University with Professor Patrick J. Wolfe.

My work runs along four connected lines: field studies of interventions in social networks, measurement and measurement error in relational data, decision-theoretic robustness for network models, and latent variable models with non-Euclidean geometry. A fuller account is on the research page.
