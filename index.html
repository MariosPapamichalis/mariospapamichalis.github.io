I am a statistician and data scientist working on two questions about complex systems: how their structure should be measured, and what happens when we intervene on them. In social networks, this means modelling how people are connected and showing that an intervention can change not only behaviour but also the ties that sustain it. In large language models, it means analysing the statistical properties of attention and token distributions, and showing that removing one component can change what the others do. I develop statistical and causal methods for both settings, built on the same principles of structure, dependence, and intervention.

I am a Postdoctoral Associate/Researcher at the Yale Institute for Network Science and the Human Nature Lab, where I work with Professor Nicholas A. Christakis on large-scale sociocentric network studies and randomized field interventions in rural Honduras. Before Yale, I was a postdoctoral researcher at Purdue University with Professor Patrick J. Wolfe, and I have collaborated with Professor Edoardo M. Airoldi. I hold an MPhil and a PhD in Statistics from University College London and and MEng and BS for the National Technical University of Athens.

Research

My work combines field data at scale with new statistical methodology, along four connected lines:

  • Interventions in social networks: how randomized programmes spread through, and reshape, the ties between people.
  • Measurement in relational data: how reporting error and missing ties distort what we learn from network surveys, and how to correct for it.
  • Robust network modelling: decision-theoretic approaches that keep inference reliable when models are misspecified.
  • Latent variable models: Bayesian models with non-Euclidean latent geometry for complex network structure.

Recent and ongoing projects include:

Statistical and causal methods for social networks

  • Evaluating how a randomized health-education intervention rewired health-advice, friendship, and financial ties (Nature, 2026).
  • Showing that cooperation in village public-goods games stabilizes near half the endowment under a moderate peer effect, with persistent group differences driven by composition rather than responsiveness.
  • Linking signed (positive and negative) network structure to anxiety, depression, and beliefs about the acceptability of wife beating.
  • Showing that social and financial ties are disproportionately directed toward physically larger people.
  • Mapping the spatial structure of friendly and antagonistic ties across 175 villages.
  • Quantifying assortativity and network change across two waves of multiplex data in 176 villages.
  • Measuring reporting error, or "forgotten ties", in network data collection.

Statistical and causal methods for identifying properties of large language models

  • Treating attention rows as compositional data, showing that standard attention metrics flip 17–47% of head-similarity verdicts depending on how the attention sink is handled, and separating sink from content with Aitchison geometry.
  • Decomposing chain-of-thought entropy into scaffolding, routing, and content channels, and showing that restated answers inflate standard evaluations of chain-of-thought compression.
  • Treating component ablation in transformers as a designed factorial experiment, showing that one-at-a-time ablation cannot identify a component's effect once its collaborators are removed, and that 6 of the 17 influential heads in the published GPT-2 indirect-object circuit change sign when their collaborators are removed.
  • Developing support-safe variational filtering for hybrid dynamical systems, which keeps learned proposals from discarding feasible regimes and enables sparse recovery of physical laws (NeurIPS, 2026).
